# Gen Z Adoption of AI in Banking: Evidence from Malaysia

### An Extended Technology Acceptance Model Using PLS-SEM

Individual bachelor's thesis examining the factors that influence Malaysian Gen Z's intention to adopt AI in banking using primary survey data and Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM).

**Original thesis title:** *Banking 4.0 in Malaysia: Is Gen Z Ready for It?*

---

## Project Overview

This thesis investigates the behavioural factors influencing Malaysian Gen Z's intention to adopt Artificial Intelligence (AI) in banking.

The study extends the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) by incorporating **perceived risk** and **perceived trust**, alongside traditional TAM constructs such as perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use and attitude towards technology.

Primary survey data were collected from **633 Malaysian Gen Z respondents** and analysed using **SmartPLS 4** with PLS-SEM.

The research evaluates both the measurement model and structural model, including reliability, validity, mediation effects, path significance and explanatory power.

---

## Research Question

The study examines how the following factors shape Malaysian Gen Z's intention to adopt AI in banking:

- Perceived Usefulness (PU)
- Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU)
- Attitude Towards AI in Banking (ATT)
- Perceived Risk (PR)
- Perceived Trust (PT)

The dependent variable is:

- Intention to Adopt AI in Banking (INT)

---

## Theoretical Framework

The study is grounded primarily in the **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)** and extends it using risk- and trust-related considerations.

The framework also draws on **Prospect Theory** to interpret the relative influence of perceived benefits and perceived risks in technology-adoption decisions.

---

## Data

The study uses primary, cross-sectional survey data collected from Malaysian Gen Z respondents.

### Main Study

- **633 valid responses**
- 5-point Likert-scale questionnaire
- Demographic and behavioural variables
- Six latent constructs

### Pilot Study

A separate **34-response pilot study** was conducted before the main survey.

Cronbach's alpha was used to assess internal consistency, and all constructs exceeded the acceptable reliability threshold.

---

## Methodology

The empirical analysis was conducted using **SmartPLS 4** and PLS-SEM.

### 1. Measurement Model Assessment

The measurement model was assessed using:

- Indicator loadings
- Internal consistency reliability
- Composite reliability
- Convergent validity
- Average Variance Extracted (AVE)
- Discriminant validity
- Heterotrait-Monotrait ratio (HTMT)

### 2. Structural Model Assessment

The structural model was assessed using:

- Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)
- Path coefficients
- Statistical significance
- Bootstrapping
- Effect sizes
- R-squared
- Mediation analysis

Bootstrapping was performed using **5,000 subsamples**.

---

## Hypotheses

Six relationships were tested:

| Hypothesis | Relationship | Result |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | PU → INT | Supported |
| H2 | PU → ATT → INT | Supported |
| H3 | PEOU → ATT → INT | Supported |
| H4 | PEOU → PU → INT | Supported |
| H5 | PR → ATT → INT | Supported |
| H6 | PT → ATT → INT | Supported |

All six hypothesised relationships were statistically significant.

---

## Key Results

The structural model explains approximately **59.9% of the variance** in intention to adopt AI in banking.

### Total Effects on Adoption Intention

| Factor | Total Effect |
|---|---:|
| Attitude Towards AI in Banking | **0.633** |
| Perceived Usefulness | **0.475** |
| Perceived Ease of Use | **0.385** |
| Perceived Trust | **0.190** |
| Perceived Risk | **-0.043** |

The results indicate that **attitude towards AI in banking is the strongest predictor of adoption intention**.

Perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use also have substantial positive effects.

Perceived trust has a positive influence, while perceived risk has a statistically significant but comparatively small negative effect.

---

## Effect Sizes

Selected structural-model effect sizes include:

| Relationship | f² |
|---|---:|
| ATT → INT | **0.486** |
| PEOU → PU | **0.614** |
| PU → ATT | **0.305** |
| PT → ATT | **0.146** |
| PU → INT | **0.040** |
| PEOU → ATT | **0.031** |
| PR → ATT | **0.011** |

The largest effects are observed for:

- Attitude → Intention
- Perceived Ease of Use → Perceived Usefulness

---

## Key Findings

- Attitude is the strongest factor influencing intention to adopt AI in banking.
- Perceived usefulness has both direct and indirect positive effects on adoption intention.
- Perceived ease of use affects adoption indirectly through attitude and perceived usefulness.
- Perceived trust positively influences intention through attitude.
- Perceived risk negatively influences adoption intention through attitude, but the magnitude of the effect is relatively small.
- All six proposed hypotheses are supported.
- The extended TAM provides moderate explanatory power for Gen Z banking-AI adoption intention.

---

## Practical Implications

The findings suggest that banks and financial-service providers should prioritise:

- Demonstrating the practical usefulness of AI-enabled banking services
- Designing simple and intuitive customer experiences
- Strengthening customer trust in AI-enabled financial services
- Communicating security, privacy and risk controls clearly
- Improving Gen Z's overall attitude towards banking AI

The results imply that adoption strategies should focus not only on reducing risk but also on enhancing perceived value, usability and trust.

---

## Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings:

- The study uses convenience sampling, creating potential sampling bias.
- The respondent pool is heavily concentrated among tertiary students.
- The study does not distinguish clearly between existing banking-AI adopters and non-adopters.
- Other behavioural factors may be omitted from the extended TAM framework.
- The cross-sectional design captures perceptions at one point in time.
- The results should not be interpreted as strong causal evidence.
- Generalisability beyond Malaysian Gen Z respondents is limited.

## Key Takeaway

This thesis demonstrates the application of primary-data research and PLS-SEM to a contemporary financial-technology question.

The results suggest that Gen Z adoption of AI in banking is driven primarily by attitude, usefulness and ease of use, while trust also plays an important role and perceived risk has a smaller negative influence.

The project combines financial-services research, behavioural modelling, statistical analysis and practical interpretation for banks and policymakers.
